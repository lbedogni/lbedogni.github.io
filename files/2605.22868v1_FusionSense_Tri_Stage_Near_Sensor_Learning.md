## 📌 Summary & Core Contributions
*   **The Problem**: Multimodal sensor suites (cameras, LiDAR, depth, etc.) are proliferating at the edge. Prior split-computing approaches either (1) transmit all raw sensor streams to powerful servers to fuse them, blowing up energy/latency budgets, or (2) filter sensor data locally with uni-modal filters that ignore cross-modal dependencies (leading to missed events or redundant transmissions).
*   **The Proposed Solution**: **FusionSense**, a fusion-aware, intelligent near-sensor sensing framework for energy-constrained autonomous edge systems. 
*   **Methodology**: Uses a tri-stage training approach:
    1.  *Server-side*: A server-side fusion model learns the downstream task.
    2.  *Filter-out-Safe (FoS) labels*: Quantifies each modality's downstream necessity relative to the fused decision.
    3.  *Edge-side*: Compacters train lightweight near-sensor classifiers by injecting near-sensor predictions as auxiliary signals, establishing a runtime decision layer that scales linearly with sensor count.
*   **Key Results**: 
    *   Sustains downstream task quality at substantially higher data-reduction rates compared to uni-modal filters.
    *   Achieves up to **33x lower energy** at 1% FoI prevalence (11x at 10% prevalence).
    *   Provides a **92.3% reduction in quality loss** at a fixed 30% data reduction compared to prior filtering baselines.

## 🔍 Critical Analysis & Relevance to Luca's Research
*   **Core Split Computing Relevance**: Directly addresses "what to compute locally vs. what to transmit" under a multimodal setting. Rather than statically partitioning a single network, it dynamically decides modality transmissions based on cross-modal downstream necessity.
*   **Edge Inference Efficiency**: This is a major breakthrough for multi-sensor IoT/Edge nodes (like robotic/autonomous systems with cameras and LiDAR), demonstrating how near-sensor lightweight classifiers can acts as smart gatekeepers using feedback/distillation from server-side fusion representations.
