# The Three Dimensions of ROS 2 Middleware

## Summary
This survey analyzes ROS 2 middleware (DDS, Zenoh) through three structural dimensions: **Space, Time, and State**.
- **Space:** Spatial abstraction from physical topology to enable modular deployment.
- **Time:** Temporal predictability for control loops.
- **State:** Contextual continuity despite dynamic node participation and intermittent connectivity.

The paper identifies fundamental trade-offs: spatial abstraction can weaken temporal guarantees, and preserving state continuity increases overhead that competes with time-critical communication.

## Analysis
While not a "Split Computing" paper, this is foundational for **Distributed Edge Computing** in robotics. The "Space, Time, State" framework is a useful lens for analyzing any distributed inference system where the "nodes" (sensors, edge servers, cloud) are dynamic and connectivity is unreliable.

## Key Takeaways
- There is a structural conflict between abstraction (Space) and predictability (Time) in distributed robotic middleware.
- State management is the primary bottleneck for scalable, robust robotic edge systems.
