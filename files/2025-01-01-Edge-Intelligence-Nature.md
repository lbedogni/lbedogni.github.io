# Edge intelligence through in-sensor and near-sensor computing for the artificial intelligence of things

## Summary
This perspective paper discusses the shift from centralized computing (CPU/GPU) to decentralized "sensor edge" computing to overcome bottlenecks in data transfer, energy consumption, and latency.

## Key Concepts
- **In-sensor Computing**: Processing is embedded directly within sensor pixels. It uses analog compute-in-memory (CIM) technologies (memristors, FETs) to perform multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations in the analog domain, eliminating the need for power-hungry analog-to-digital converters (ADCs).
- **Near-sensor Computing**: Dedicated computing units are placed immediately adjacent to the sensors to minimize data movement across physical interfaces.
- **Biological Inspiration**: The approach mimics biological sensory systems (like the human retina) that pre-process information at the interface before sending it to the brain.

## Results & Analysis
- **Benefits**: Significant reductions in energy consumption, latency, and bandwidth. Improved data privacy and real-time decision-making.
- **Relevance**: Directly relates to Distributed Edge Inference and Split Computing by pushing the "split" point to the very edge of the sensing hardware.
