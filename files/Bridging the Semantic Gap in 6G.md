# Bridging the Semantic Gap in 6G: Tiny Language Models Under the Latency-Accuracy-Size Trilemma

## Summary
This review focuses on the role of Tiny Language Models (t-LMs) in enabling semantic communication for 6G wireless networks. The central goal is to transmit "meaning" rather than bits, reducing bandwidth usage.

## Key Findings
- **Latency-Accuracy-Size Trilemma**: Constrained IoT devices struggle with the memory and energy demands of large transformers.
- **Compression Gains**: Survey shows that model compression (quantization, pruning, etc.) can reduce encoder size by up to **99.98%** while maintaining task accuracy.
- **Split Computing**: Enables device-side encoders with as few as 640 parameters.
- **Energy Savings**: Knowledge graph integration can reduce transmission energy by 65%.

## Analysis
The paper highlights a shift towards AI-native infrastructure where the "semantic gap" is bridged by task-specialized, compact models. For Luca's interest in Distributed Edge Inference, the mention of split computing as a key compression paradigm is highly relevant.
