# Future of Edge AI in biodiversity monitoring

**Authors:** Aude Vuilliomenet, Kate E. Jones, Duncan Wilson
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13496
**Date:** 2026-02 (approx)

## Summary
This review paper analyzes 82 studies published between 2017 and 2025 that apply edge computing to biodiversity monitoring across acoustic, vision-based, tracking, and multi-modal systems. It highlights the shift from a traditional "collect-upload-analyse" workflow to autonomous, responsive sensing systems.

## Key Insights
- **BLERP Framework**: The authors frame the advantages of edge AI through **B**andwidth, **L**atency, **E**conomic cost, **R**eliability, and **P**rivacy.
- **Hardware Spectrum**:
    - **MCUs**: Low-power, suitable for single-taxon or rare-event detection.
    - **SBCs**: More capable, supporting multi-species classification and real-time alerts.
- **Evolution**: Systems have evolved from proof-of-concept to robust tools, but implementation remains fragmented.
- **Recommendation**: Strong need for collaboration between ecologists, engineers, and data scientists to align AI model design with field constraints and ecological questions.

## Relevance to Luca's Research
Highly relevant to **IoT** and **Edge Computing**. While focused on biodiversity, the discussion on hardware trade-offs (MCU vs SBC) and the BLERP framework are generalizable to other edge inference scenarios.
