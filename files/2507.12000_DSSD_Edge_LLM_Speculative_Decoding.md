# Efficient Edge-Device LLM Deployment and Collaborative Inference via Distributed Split Speculative Decoding (DSSD)

**arXiv ID:** 2507.12000
**Date:** July 2025
**Venue:** ICML 2025
**Topic:** LLM Inference, Speculative Decoding, Edge Computing, Distributed Inference

## Summary
The paper proposes **Distributed Split Speculative Decoding (DSSD)**, a novel architecture for deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) across device-edge systems. 

### The Problem
Collaborative frameworks often use a Small Language Model (SLM) on the device to draft tokens and a larger LLM at the edge to verify them (Speculative Decoding). However, existing methods suffer from:
1. **High Uplink Costs:** Verifying candidate tokens often requires transmitting large vocabulary distributions from the device to the edge.
2. **Latency-Accuracy Trade-off:** Efforts to reduce latency often lead to a drop in inference quality.

### The Solution: DSSD
DSSD optimizes the verification phase by partitioning it between the device and the edge. Instead of sending multiple distributions uplink, DSSD utilizes a mechanism that replaces this with a single downlink transmission. 

### Key Results
- **Reduced Communication Latency:** Significantly lowers the uplink overhead associated with token verification.
- **Maintained Quality:** Preserves the inference accuracy of the target LLM.
- **Outperforms Baselines:** Shows superior performance compared to existing SLM-LLM collaborative frameworks.

## Analysis & Relevance to Luca's Research
This paper is highly relevant to **Distributed Edge Inference** and **Split Computing**.

- **Innovation in Verification:** Most split computing research focuses on where to split the model layers. DSSD focuses on the *communication pattern* of the verification step in speculative decoding.
- **Asymmetric Communication:** It explicitly leverages the asymmetry of edge networks (downlink $\gg$ uplink), which is a critical real-world constraint in IoT/Edge environments.
- **Synergy with Split Computing:** This is a specialized form of split computing where the "split" is not just a layer but a functional stage of the decoding process.
