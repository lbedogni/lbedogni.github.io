# Profiling-Driven Adaptive Distributed Transformer Inference on Embedded Edge Deployment

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25682
**Date:** 2026-05-25
**Tags:** #edge-computing #distributed-inference #transformer #nvidia-jetson

## Summary
This paper investigates the practical performance of distributing Transformer inference across embedded edge devices (specifically NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano) using WiFi. It reveals that real-world bottlenecks are often overlooked by simulations, specifically the CPU-GPU staging overhead during communication on SoC architectures.

To mitigate this, the authors propose **Prism**, which combines:
1. **Segment Means Compression**: To reduce the volume of data exchanged.
2. **Lightweight Offline Profiling**: To adaptively decide at runtime whether to execute locally or distribute the workload.

## Analysis
The key insight is the "performance paradox" where distributed execution can be slower than local execution due to the lack of high-speed interconnects (like NVLink) on embedded devices. Prism's adaptive strategy reduces latency by 65-77% and energy by 34-52% compared to static distributed setups. This is a critical contribution for moving distributed LLM/Transformer inference from theory to embedded reality.

## Relevance to Research
Directly addresses the "Distributed Edge Inference" and "Edge Computing" themes, emphasizing hardware-aware adaptation.
